Eight puzzles

One idea in every game.

Each game has a daily board on every grade, random boards whenever you want more, and a short demo the first time you open it. Every board has a single solution that logic reaches.

  • Sudoku

    The classic, nine by nine. Six grades from Beginner to Extreme, each defined by the techniques a board needs, not by a score.

  • Tenbin

    Fill the grid with two symbols. Never three alike in a row, each line half and half, and no two lines the same.

  • Light Up

    Place bulbs until every white cell is lit. Bulbs can't light each other, and a number tells how many bulbs touch it.

  • Star Battle

    One star in every row, column and region, and stars never touch, not even at a corner. Under the ellipsis menu on its card, Two stars has boards with two stars in each row, column and region, ten by ten and twelve by twelve.

  • Killer Sudoku

    Sudoku by sums: the digits in a cage add up to the number in its corner and never repeat. Easy and Medium start with a few digits, Hard with none. With Kata Pro.

Three more with Kata Pro. In Pipes you turn the knobs until every pipe joins one net from the ink well, and the net never closes a loop. In Ink Levels you fill tanks with ink until every number fits the ink squares in its row or column. In Nonogram you fill squares with ink until every row and column matches its numbers. Hints that explain

Three steps. Stop at the one you need.

  1. The name of the technique. Often that is enough to see it yourself.
  2. Where to look. The row, column, box, cage or region the move is in.
  3. The proof. The exact cells that make the move necessary, marked on the board.

A hint first points out entries that don't fit, before it suggests anything new. In sudoku it works from your own notes when they are sound, and a wrong digit gets a Why not? that follows it a few steps until the board contradicts itself.
